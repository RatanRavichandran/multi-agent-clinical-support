# Case Study: 32-Year-Old Male with Acute Traumatic Head Injury

## Patient Information

**Demographics:**
- 32-year-old male
- Construction worker
- Married with one child

**Chief Complaint:**
- Found unconscious at construction site after falling from scaffolding (approximately 3 meters)
- Witnessed brief period of consciousness followed by deterioration

## History of Present Illness

Patient was working at a construction site when he reportedly lost his balance and fell approximately 3 meters from scaffolding, striking the right side of his head on a metal beam during the fall. Co-workers report he was initially unconscious for approximately 2 minutes, then regained consciousness and was oriented but complained of severe headache. Approximately 20 minutes later, he became progressively drowsy and eventually unresponsive, prompting emergency services activation. EMS found patient with GCS of 8 (E2V2M4) at the scene. Patient was intubated for airway protection and transported to the Emergency Department.

## Assessment and Plan

**Assessment:**
32-year-old male construction worker with traumatic head injury following a fall from height, presenting with:
1. Large right temporoparietal acute extradural hematoma (approximately 35mm thickness)
2. Associated right temporal bone fracture
3. Significant mass effect with 9mm midline shift
4. GCS 7T (E2V1TM4) and deteriorating neurological status
5. Evidence of elevated intracranial pressure (right pupillary dilation, Cushing's triad)

The CT findings are consistent with acute extradural hematoma rather than subdural hematoma based on the following radiographic features:
- Biconvex (lentiform) shape
- Sharply demarcated margins
- Limited by cranial sutures
- Association with skull fracture
- Homogeneous hyperdensity

The clinical presentation is classic for extradural hematoma with a "lucid interval" (initial consciousness followed by deterioration). The most likely source of bleeding is the right middle meningeal artery, secondary to temporal bone fracture.

**Plan:**

1. **Immediate Neurosurgical Intervention:**
   - Emergency right temporoparietal craniotomy for evacuation of extradural hematoma
   - Identification and control of bleeding vessel (likely middle meningeal artery)
   - ICP monitor placement

2. **Perioperative Management:**
   - Continue mechanical ventilation with target PaCO2 35-38 mmHg
   - Mannitol 1g/kg IV bolus to reduce intracranial pressure
   - Hypertonic saline (3% NaCl) 250mL IV over 15 minutes
   - Elevate head of bed to 30 degrees
   - Maintain MAP >80 mmHg to ensure adequate cerebral perfusion pressure
   - Levetiracetam 1000mg IV loading dose followed by 500mg IV BID for seizure prophylaxis
   - Type and cross for 2 units packed red blood cells

3. **Post-operative Care:**
   - Admission to Neurosurgical ICU
   - Repeat head CT within 6 hours post-operation
   - Hourly neurological checks
   - ICP goal <20 mmHg
   - Cerebral perfusion pressure goal >60 mmHg
   - Consider early tracheostomy if prolonged mechanical ventilation anticipated
   - DVT prophylaxis with sequential compression devices, consider chemical prophylaxis 24 hours post-operation if no evidence of continued bleeding

4. **Family Discussion:**
   - Explain diagnosis, management plan, and prognosis to family
   - Discuss risks of surgery including bleeding, infection, stroke, and mortality
   - Review expected post-operative course and potential need for rehabilitation

5. **Long-term Planning:**
   - Neurocognitive assessment once patient is stabilized
   - Physical therapy, occupational therapy, and speech therapy consultations as needed
   - Outpatient neurosurgery follow-up in 2 weeks
   - Return to work evaluation in 3-6 months based on recovery
