# Case Study: 42-Year-Old Female with Acute Right Upper Quadrant Pain

## Patient Information

**Demographics:**
- 42-year-old female
- Elementary school teacher
- Married with two children

**Chief Complaint:**
- Severe right upper quadrant pain that began 8 hours ago
- Nausea and vomiting

## History of Present Illness

Patient presents to the emergency department with acute onset of severe, constant right upper quadrant pain that began after dinner approximately 8 hours ago. She describes the pain as sharp, non-radiating, and rated 8/10 in severity. The pain is exacerbated by movement and deep inspiration. She reports associated nausea with two episodes of vomiting of bilious content. She denies fever but feels "warm." The patient reports similar but milder episodes over the past 3 months, typically occurring after consuming fatty meals, which would resolve spontaneously within a few hours. This current episode is more severe and has not improved with acetaminophen or position changes. She denies any change in bowel habits, jaundice, or pruritus.

## Past Medical History

- Hyperlipidemia diagnosed 2 years ago
- Obesity (BMI 31)
- Cesarean delivery for both pregnancies
- Migraine headaches

## Medications

- Simvastatin 20mg daily
- Multivitamin daily
- Sumatriptan as needed for migraines
- Acetaminophen as needed for pain

## Allergies

- No known drug allergies

## Family History

- Mother with cholelithiasis, underwent cholecystectomy at age 48
- Father with hypertension
- Sister with hypothyroidism
- No family history of gastrointestinal malignancies

## Social History

- Never smoker
- Alcohol: Occasional glass of wine (1-2 per week)
- Occupation: Elementary school teacher
- Diet: Self-described as "unhealthy" with frequent fast food consumption
- Exercise: Minimal physical activity
- Married with two children (ages 10 and 12)

## Review of Systems

- **Constitutional:** Reports generalized malaise, denies fever, chills, or recent weight changes
- **HEENT:** No scleral icterus, no headache
- **Cardiovascular:** No chest pain, no palpitations
- **Respiratory:** No shortness of breath, no cough
- **Gastrointestinal:** Positive for RUQ pain, nausea, vomiting; denies diarrhea, constipation, or changes in stool color
- **Genitourinary:** No dysuria, no hematuria
- **Musculoskeletal:** No joint pain or swelling
- **Neurological:** No dizziness, no focal weakness
- **Skin:** No jaundice, no rashes or pruritus

## Physical Examination

**Vital Signs:**
- Blood Pressure: 138/84 mmHg
- Heart Rate: 92 bpm, regular
- Respiratory Rate: 18 breaths/min
- Temperature: 38.1°C
- Oxygen Saturation: 98% on room air
- BMI: 31 kg/m²

**General:** Moderately distressed female, visibly uncomfortable, lying still in bed

**HEENT:** Normocephalic, atraumatic, sclerae non-icteric, mucous membranes moist

**Neck:** Supple, no lymphadenopathy, no thyromegaly

**Cardiovascular:** Regular rate and rhythm, normal S1 and S2, no murmurs, rubs, or gallops

**Respiratory:** Clear to auscultation bilaterally, no wheezes, rales, or rhonchi

**Abdominal:** Obese, soft, marked tenderness to palpation in right upper quadrant with voluntary guarding. Positive Murphy's sign on clinical examination. No rebound tenderness. No palpable masses or organomegaly. Normal bowel sounds. No costovertebral angle tenderness.

**Extremities:** No edema, pulses 2+ and equal bilaterally

**Skin:** No jaundice, rashes, or spider angiomata

**Neurological:** Alert and oriented x3, cranial nerves intact, normal strength and sensation

## Diagnostic Studies

**Right Upper Quadrant Ultrasound:**
- **Gallbladder:** Moderately distended with thickened wall measuring 4.5 mm (normal <3 mm). Multiple hyperechoic foci with posterior acoustic shadowing consistent with gallstones, largest measuring 1.2 cm. One stone impacted at the gallbladder neck. Pericholecystic fluid present.
- **Sonographic Murphy's Sign:** Positive - patient reported maximal tenderness when the ultrasound probe was directly over the gallbladder.
- **Common Bile Duct:** Measured at 6.8 mm in diameter (upper limit of normal: 7 mm). No intraluminal filling defects visualized.
- **Liver:** Normal in size and echogenicity. No focal lesions.
- **Pancreas:** Visualized portions appear normal.
- **Kidneys:** Normal in size and echogenicity. No hydronephrosis.
- **Conclusion:** Findings consistent with acute cholecystitis with cholelithiasis. Common bile duct at upper limit of normal.

## Assessment and Plan

**Assessment:**
42-year-old female with acute cholecystitis with cholelithiasis based on clinical presentation, laboratory findings, and ultrasound results demonstrating:
1. Acute onset severe right upper quadrant pain
2. Positive Murphy's sign on clinical exam and ultrasound
3. Low-grade fever and leukocytosis
4. Ultrasound findings of gallbladder wall thickening, gallstones (including one impacted at the neck), and pericholecystic fluid
5. Mildly elevated liver enzymes and bilirubin
6. Common bile duct at upper limit of normal (6.8 mm)

**Differential Diagnosis:**
- Acute cholecystitis (most likely)
- Choledocholithiasis (less likely given CBD size but cannot be excluded)
- Acute hepatitis
- Peptic ulcer disease
- Right-sided pyelonephritis

**Plan:**

1. **Immediate Management:**
   - NPO status
   - IV fluid resuscitation with normal saline
   - IV antibiotics: Piperacillin-tazobactam 3.375g IV every 6 hours
   - Pain management: Morphine 4mg IV every 4 hours as needed
   - Antiemetic: Ondansetron 4mg IV every 6 hours as needed
   - Serial abdominal exams

2. **Surgical Consultation:**
   - Immediate general surgery consultation for likely laparoscopic cholecystectomy
   - Consider MRCP if common bile duct obstruction becomes more concerning (CBD dilation increases, bilirubin continues to rise)

3. **Further Laboratory Testing:**
   - Follow liver function tests and WBC count every 12 hours
   - Consider additional biliary imaging if bilirubin continues to increase

4. **Disposition:**
   - Admit to general surgery service
   - Anticipated surgical intervention within 24-48 hours

5. **Supportive Care:**
   - Anticoagulation prophylaxis with sequential compression devices
   - Early ambulation as tolerated
   - Continue home medications except simvastatin (hold during acute illness)

6. **Patient Education:**
   - Surgical procedure explanation and informed consent
   - Post-operative expectations
   - Dietary modifications following cholecystectomy
   - Long-term follow-up planning
