## General Principles & Radiation Safety (1-10)

1. Balance diagnostic yield against radiation risk before approving any study (ALARA).【turn4view0】  
2. Obtain informed consent when high-dose or iodinated/gadolinium contrast is planned.【turn3view0】  
3. Understand deterministic vs stochastic effects of ionising radiation to counsel patients properly.【turn4view0】  
4. Record cumulative dose, especially in paediatric and oncology follow-up cohorts.【turn3view0】  
5. Shield thyroid, gonads, and eyes whenever coverage will not obscure the anatomy of interest.【turn3view0】  
6. In pregnancy, prioritise US or MRI; X-ray/CT only if imaging is life-saving.【turn5view0】  
7. MRI in the first trimester is reserved for essential, non-deferrable indications.【turn5view0】  
8. Screen renal function, metformin use, asthma, prior contrast reaction pre-iodine study.【turn3view0】  
9. Pre-medicate prior severe reactors with steroid + antihistamine regimen 12 h beforehand.【turn3view0】  
10. Note: low-osmolar agents cut severe reaction risk from ≈ 12 % to ≈ 3 %.【turn3view0】

## Modality Strengths & Contraindications (11-20)

11. **Ultrasound** is safe; minimise Doppler dwell time to reduce thermal index.【turn3view0】  
12. **CT** offers rapid anatomic overview but warrants dose tracking in young patients.【turn4view0】  
13. **MRI** gives unrivalled soft-tissue contrast; absolute contra-indicated with most pacemakers, unknown clips, cochlear/penile implants.【turn5view0】  
14. **Angiography/IR** is key for drainage, embolisation, angioplasty; avoid elective use post-acute MI or in severe renal failure without hydration plan.【turn3view0】  
15. **Nuclear medicine** provides physiology; pregnancy is a firm contraindication unless benefit outweighs risk.【turn3view0】  
16. **Mammography** rarely indicated under 25 y; radiation-induced cancer risk ≈ 1 in 1 million per exam.【turn3view0】  
17. **Barium studies** useful for dysphagia/motility; never use when perforation is suspected (use Gastrografin).【turn3view0】  
18. **IVU** still first-line for renal colic when US is equivocal and low-dose CT unavailable.【turn3view0】  
19. **Gadolinium** reactions are rarer than iodine but observe patients post-administration.【turn5view0】  
20. Consult a radiologist early when planning NM or IR procedures to optimise protocol and after-care.【turn3view0】

## Head & Neck (21-30)

21. Solitary thyroid nodule → ultrasound ± FNAC first line.【turn5view0】  
22. CT neck/chest if thyroid mass suspected to extend retrosternally.【turn5view0】  
23. Radionuclide scan supports metastatic survey, not primary therapy decision, in solitary nodule.【turn5view0】  
24. Diffuse goitre → plain X-ray for tracheal deviation; US for nodularity.【turn5view0】  
25. Hyperthyroidism mapping uses Tc-99m or I-123 scintigraphy for hot nodules.【turn5view0】  
26. CT paranasal sinuses is pre-FESS gold standard; MRI clarifies fungal/soft tissue lesion.【turn5view0】  
27. Plain sinus films detect air–fluid but miss osteomeatal disease.【turn5view0】  
28. Non-traumatic epistaxis with mass suspicion → CT/MRI then angiography for embolisation planning.【turn5view0】  
29. Facial trauma: CT axial + coronal + 3-D recon replaces plain films for fracture mapping.【turn5view0】  
30. Ocular US assesses lens dislocation or IOFB when globes intact in facial trauma.【turn5view0】

## CNS (31-40)

31. Suspected ICH → non-contrast CT immediately.【turn5view0】  
32. MRI/MRA surpass CT for brain-stem and IAC lesions (e.g., acoustic neuroma).【turn5view0】  
33. Catheter angiography remains definitive for aneurysm/AVM localisation.【turn5view0】  
34. Minor head injury → CT if neuro deficit, age > 65, or anticoagulated.【turn1search3】  
35. Skull X-ray alone cannot exclude intracranial injury; do not rely on it.【turn1search1】  
36. First adult seizure → MRI epilepsy protocol recommended.【turn5view0】  
37. Vertigo/hearing loss with acoustic neuroma suspicion → MRI IACs.【turn5view0】  
38. Acute stroke LVO triage → CT angiography before thrombectomy.【turn1search12】  
39. LP follows negative CT when SAH suspicion persists clinically.【turn1search12】  
40. Uncomplicated migraine with normal exam needs no imaging.【turn5view0】

## Cardiovascular (41-48)

41. Equivocal MI work-up → ECG-gated CT coronary angiography if troponin borderline.【turn4view0】  
42. Suspected aortic dissection → contrast CT chest/abdomen; MRI when stable & iodine-contraindicated.【turn4view0】  
43. Pulmonary embolism: CTPA first; V/Q scan in pregnancy or renal failure.【turn4view0】  
44. AAA screening → ultrasound; CT-angiography for > 5.5 cm or symptomatic aneurysm pre-EVAR.【turn4view0】  
45. PAD claudication → duplex US; MR/CT angiography for runoff mapping pre-surgery.【turn4view0】  
46. DVT → compression US groin-to-calf standard.【turn4view0】  
47. Secondary hypertension → renal Doppler; catheter angiography if FMD suspected.【turn4view0】  
48. Neurogenic claudication suggests spinal MRI rather than vascular imaging.【turn4view0】

## Musculoskeletal (49-56)

49. AVN hip early detection → MRI femoral heads.【turn4view0】  
50. Stress fractures: MRI or bone scan when radiographs negative.【turn4view0】  
51. Osteomyelitis: MRI better than CT for marrow involvement & soft tissue.【turn4view0】  
52. Bone tumour work-up: X-ray → CT for cortex → MRI for marrow & soft-tissue extent.【turn4view0】  
53. Soft-tissue mass: MRI + contrast before biopsy avoids tract contamination.【turn4view0】  
54. Shoulder pain: X-ray; US for rotator cuff; MRI if operative plan.【turn4view0】  
55. Cervical spine trauma: multi-detector CT supplants plain lateral view.【turn4view0】  
56. Mechanical low-back pain without red flags: no imaging ≤ 6 weeks.【turn4view0】

## Respiratory (57-64)

57. Chest trauma: initial CXR + FAST; CT thorax for penetrating or unstable blunt trauma.【turn4view0】  
58. Bronchiectasis evaluation → high-resolution CT thorax.【turn4view0】  
59. Haemoptysis: contrast CT chest localises bleed and rules out malignancy.【turn4view0】  
60. Solitary pulmonary nodule: thin-slice CT then PET or biopsy per risk stratification.【turn4view0】  
61. Mediastinal mass: contrast CT; MRI for vascular/spinal extension mapping.【turn4view0】  
62. Multiple nodules: HRCT + PET to differentiate metastasis vs granulomatous disease.【turn4view0】  
63. Diaphragmatic lesion: MRI differentiates cystic vs solid or congenital vs acquired mass.【turn4view0】  
64. Chronic cough with normal CXR → HRCT before bronchoscopy referral.【turn4view0】

## Gastro-intestinal (65-72)

65. Dysphagia: barium swallow first; CT/MRI if obstructive lesion suspected.【turn4view0】  
66. Dyspepsia < 45 y no alarm features → no imaging needed.【turn4view0】  
67. Acute abdomen: erect AXR + US; CT A/P with contrast if diagnosis unclear.【turn4view0】  
68. Palpable abdo mass: US for cystic vs solid; CT/MRI for staging.【turn4view0】  
69. Suspected abscess: contrast CT or US-guided aspiration ± drainage.【turn4view0】  
70. SBO: erect AXR; CT for transition point and cause.【turn4view0】  
71. GI bleed after negative endoscopy → CT angiography for active blush.【turn4view0】  
72. Blunt abdo trauma: FAST US first; CT contrast for organ injury severity.【turn4view0】

## Genito-urinary (73-80)

73. Painless haematuria ≥ 35 y → CT urography.【turn4view0】  
74. Indeterminate renal mass: US then multiphasic CT/MRI (Bosniak staging).【turn4view0】  
75. Recurrent adult UTI: US for obstruction; CT IVU for structural anomalies.【turn4view0】  
76. Renal trauma grading: contrast CT kidneys/ureters/bladder.【turn4view0】  
77. Acute renal failure: US for size/obstruction; Doppler if vascular cause suspected.【turn4view0】  
78. Post-transplant dysfunction: Doppler US; nuclear renogram for perfusion split.【turn4view0】  
79. LUTS with enlarged prostate: TRUS volume; MRI if PSA high or repeat negative biopsy.【turn4view0】  
80. Acute scrotal pain: Doppler US distinguishes torsion (no flow) from epididymo-orchitis (hyperaemia).【turn4view0】

## Female Reproductive (81-87)

81. 1st-trimester bleeding: TVUS for viability & ectopic check.【turn4view0】  
82. 3rd-trimester bleeding: obstetric US + placental Doppler mapping.【turn4view0】  
83. Equivocal ectopic: serial β-hCG + repeat TVUS; MRI pelvis if still ambiguous.【turn4view0】  
84. Suspected IUGR: serial growth biometry + umbilical artery Doppler.【turn4view0】  
85. Adnexal mass: TVUS; MRI for indeterminate or surgical planning.【turn4view0】  
86. Abnormal uterine bleeding > 35 y: TVUS ± SIS; MRI if focal lesion suspected.【turn4view0】  
87. Infertility work-up: HSG for tubes; TVUS for follicles/ovulation tracking.【turn4view0】

## Breast (88-90)

88. Palpable mass ≥ 35 y: bilateral 2-view mammography + targeted US (“triple assessment”).【turn3view0】  
89. Pathologic nipple discharge: mammogram + US; consider ductography for intraductal papilloma.【turn3view0】  
90. Screening mammography biennially 50-69 y; earlier if high hereditary risk pattern identified.【turn3view0】

## Paediatrics (91-100)

91. First unprovoked seizure with abnormal neuro exam → MRI brain.【turn4view0】  
92. Pediatric headache with red flags → neuro-imaging (CT/MRI) as indicated.【turn4view0】  
93. Developmental delay: MRI brain for malformation, myelination status.【turn4view0】  
94. Stridor: neck US; CT neck/chest if airway mass suspected.【turn4view0】  
95. Recurrent pneumonia: chest HRCT for bronchiectasis/congenital anomalies.【turn4view0】  
96. Projectile vomiting infant: US pylorus (HPS).【turn4view0】  
97. Prolonged neonatal jaundice → US biliary tree to exclude atresia.【turn4view0】  
98. Abdominal mass child: US first; CT/MRI for Wilms/neuroblastoma staging.【turn4view0】  
99. Febrile UTI < 2 y: renal US; MCU if VUR suspected.【turn4view0】  
100. Limping child: pelvic/knee X-ray; MRI hip if transient synovitis vs septic arthritis unclear.【turn4view0】
