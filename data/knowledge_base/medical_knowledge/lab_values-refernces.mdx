# Radiology Reference Values Guide

## CT Imaging Parameters

| Parameter | Normal Reference Range | Clinical Significance |
|-----------|-------------------------|-----------------------|
| CTDIvol (Head) | 50-75 mGy [1][8] | Elevated: Increased radiation exposure risk, requires protocol optimization. Values above 75 mGy may indicate non-compliance with ALARA principles [6][8]. |
| CTDIvol (Chest) | 10-30 mGy [1][6] | Elevated in pulmonary angiography: May suggest inadequate dose modulation. Values >30 mGy in routine chest CT warrant protocol review [8]. |
| DLP (Abdomen-Pelvis) | 500-1500 mGy·cm [6][8] | Increased dose-length product correlates with cumulative radiation exposure. DLP >1500 mGy·cm requires justification for multiphase studies [1][6]. |
| SSDE (Pediatric) | 2.4-26 mGy [8] | Size-specific dose estimate must be adjusted for patient effective diameter. Exceeding 26 mGy in children necessitates immediate protocol reassessment [8]. |

## MRI Quality Metrics

| Parameter | Optimal Range | Clinical Implications |
|-----------|---------------|-----------------------|
| SNR (1.5T Brain) | ≥20:1 [2][7] | Low SNR (<15:1) results in poor gray-white matter differentiation. Affected by voxel size (0.5-1mm³ optimal), TR (2000-3000ms), and TE (80-120ms) [2][7]. |
| SAR (Whole Body) | ≤4 W/kg [2] | Specific absorption rate exceeding 4 W/kg risks thermal injury. Reduced using low transmitter bandwidth sequences [2]. |
| Magnetic Field Homogeneity | <0.5 ppm [7] | Inhomogeneity >1 ppm causes geometric distortion, particularly problematic in fMRI and diffusion imaging [7]. |

## Ultrasound Safety Indices

| Index | Safe Threshold | Clinical Protocol |
|-------|----------------|-------------------|
| Mechanical Index (MI) | <0.7 [3] | >0.3 requires reduced scanning time in neonatal studies. >0.7 contraindicated with ultrasound contrast agents [3]. |
| Thermal Index (TIS) | <1.0 [3] | >0.7 mandates limited fetal exposure time. >1.0 prohibited in ocular ultrasound [3]. |
| Spatial Peak Temporal Average (Abdominal) | ≤720 mW/cm² [3] | Exceeding 720 mW/cm² violates FDA Track 3 guidelines except for ophthalmic applications (≤50 mW/cm²) [3]. |

## Contrast Media Characteristics

| Agent Type | Osmolality (mOsm/kg) | Adverse Reaction Rate | Clinical Considerations |
|------------|----------------------|-----------------------|-------------------------|
| Iso-osmolar Iodinated (e.g., Iodixanol) | 290 [5] | 0.2-0.4% [5] | Preferred for intrathecal/intravascular use. Lower nephrotoxicity risk [5]. |
| Low-osmolar Ionic (e.g., Ioxaglate) | 600 [5] | 0.6-1.2% [5] | Contraindicated in sickle cell crisis. Higher histamine release potential [5]. |

| Gadolinium-Based (MRI) | 1960 [2] | 0.07-2.4% [2] | NSF risk requires GBCA class monitoring (group II agents preferred for renal impairment) [2]. |
